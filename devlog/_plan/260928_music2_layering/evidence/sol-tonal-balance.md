# Tonal balance, gain, low-end ownership, and voicing evidence

28 September 2026. `V` = the opened source explicitly says it; `I` = calculation or proposed convention inferred from sources; `U` = no defensible value found. Numbers in producer tutorials are starting choices, not population measurements. All frequency shares below mean **fraction of summed 20 Hz–20 kHz linear power**, as in music2's `src/analyze/bands.tool.ts` (mono sum, Hann-window FFT power, then normalization). This is not loudness, analyzer display height, or an iZotope target value.

**Arithmetic and sensitivity model (I).** For a hypothetical continuous power spectral density `P(f)=C f^-α`, a density slope of `-s dB/octave` means `α=s/(10 log10 2)=s/3.0103`. Integrate each music2 band `[a,b]`: `W(a,b)=(b^(1-α)-a^(1-α))/(1-α)`; when `α=1`, use `ln(b/a)`. `share_i=W_i / Σ W_i` for edges 20, 60, 250, 500, 2000, 8000, 20000 Hz. A pink-noise density is `1/f`, or -3.01 dB/octave, with equal power per octave ([AES audio glossary](https://aes2.org/publications/par/n/), V). Example at -3.0: raw log widths `ln(3), ln(250/60), ln(2), ln(4), ln(4), ln(2.5)` sum to `ln(1000)=6.908`; shares are **15.7/20.5/10.0/20.1/20.2/13.4%** in sub/low/lowMid/mid/presence/air order. At -4.5 the same integral gives **43.4/30.4/8.6/10.4/5.3/1.9%**. The latter is an *invalid extrapolation* of a measured **mid-band** slope into sub and air, useful only to show how sensitive whole-band shares are to the assumed tails. Real recordings have low cutoff, bass peaks, high rolloff, kick/key-dependent lines, and often a bright percussion shelf.

The following are **illustrative full-range slope scenarios**, not empirical expectations or recommended hard limits. Each cell gives the two endpoint vectors; intermediate slopes interpolate nonlinearly. Assigning slope ranges by genre is an I-level working hypothesis from the qualitative iZotope/academic ordering above, with no fine-grained genre validation. In particular, a lo-fi low-pass and a techno bright-hat shelf violate this one-slope model.

| Scenario | Assumed full-range density slope | Computed endpoint shares, %: sub / low / lowMid / mid / presence / air | Sub+low |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pop reference | -2.7 to -3.3 dB/oct | 11.5/17.1/9.3/20.6/23.8/17.7 → 20.7/23.8/10.4/18.9/16.5/9.8 | 28.6–44.4% |
| Boom bap | -3.0 to -3.6 | 15.7/20.5/10.0/20.1/20.2/13.4 → 26.1/26.5/10.4/17.1/13.0/6.9 | 36.3–52.6% |
| Lo-fi | -3.0 to -3.6 | same broad slope span as boom bap; actual air may be lower due to filtering | 36.3–52.6% |
| House | -3.3 to -3.9 | 20.7/23.8/10.4/18.9/16.5/9.8 → 31.9/28.6/10.1/14.9/9.9/4.6 | 44.4–60.4% |
| Techno | -3.3 to -3.9 | same slope span as house; bright hats or a kick-only sub can move either tail | 44.4–60.4% |
| Trap and drill | -3.6 to -4.2 | 26.1/26.5/10.4/17.1/13.0/6.9 → 37.7/29.9/9.4/12.6/7.3/3.0 | 52.6–67.6% |

**Interpretation (I):** the existing `LOW_END_DOMINANCE` prompt at `sub+low > 0.55` can fire on a plausible bass-oriented scenario. It should trigger reference/listening review, not a claim of incorrect genre balance. The same mix can have excessive sub and insufficient 808 harmonics while passing a combined-low limit. iZotope explicitly favors reference tracks/sections over an inflexible target ([iZotope analysis](https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/why-izotope-created-the-tonal-balance-control-plug-in), V).

## 2. Gain staging and relative levels

| Claim | Status and source |
| --- | --- |
| A trap producer's *fader/track-meter starting sheet* lists kick -3 to 0 dB, 808 near 0 dB or -3 to 0 dB after processing, snare/clap -3 to -5 dB, closed hats -10 to -12 dB, melody -12 to -15 dB. The author explicitly says these are not laws. It does **not** establish comparable peak dBFS across different samples or guarantee master headroom. | V — [Louis Romani trap workflow](https://louisromani.com/blog/how-to-mix-trap-drums). |
| Another mixing studio's generic *peak* template anchors kick at -10 dBFS, snare -11 to -12 (kick minus 1–2 dB), bass/808 -12 to -14 (minus 2–4), lead -11 to -13 (minus 1–3), hats -18 to -20 (minus 8–10), pads -20 to -24 (minus 10–14). It calls the table approximate. | V — [Point Prime producer template](https://pointprimerecordings.com/blog/volume-balancing-cheat-sheet/). A single template is not a measured genre median. |
| In one melodic-techno leveling walkthrough the kick alone reads about -8 dB on the master, adding bass should raise it by around 1 dB rather than 6 dB, and the bass spectrum stays below the kick fundamental; hats support the clap/lead. These are measurements/choices from one demonstration, not house/techno norms. | V — [Production Music Live walkthrough](https://www.productionmusiclive.com/blogs/news/how-to-level-a-mix). |
| iZotope gives approximately 6 dB pre-master headroom as a general guideline; -6 to -3 dBFS on the unmastered full mix is a practical *convention*, not an audio requirement or track-level gain rule. | V for ~6 dB — [iZotope 808 guide](https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/how-to-mix-808s); V for -6 to -3 as producer prescription — [Point Prime](https://pointprimerecordings.com/blog/volume-balancing-cheat-sheet/). |
| A defensible common starting relationship is kick strongest transient; trap/drill/boom-bap 808/bass roughly 0–4 dB below kick peak, snare/clap around 0–5 below, hats 7–12 below, lead 1–6 below, pads 10–15 below. For house/techno, kick is anchor, bass roughly 2–6 below, clap 1–6 below, hats 7–13 below, lead 1–6 below, pads 8–15 below. | I — broad convention synthesized from the three producer examples above; **not** validated as typical distributions. Differences in crest factor mean equal peak does not imply equal perceived loudness. |
| 808 excess is best checked by overlap and duration as well as fader value: shorten kick decay, make a narrow dynamic cut near its fundamental (iZotope example 62 Hz), duck 808 briefly, and add upper harmonics/saturation so the note translates on smaller speakers instead of only raising sub power. | V — [iZotope worked 808 example](https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/how-to-mix-808s). |

## 3. Low-end ownership

| Claim | Status and source |
| --- | --- |
| A narrow 60–100 Hz kick-to-bass duck of 2–3 dB and recovery around 80–150 ms at 120 BPM is one Master's The Mix product author's transparent starting example. It is *frequency-selective*, so the same depth applied to the whole 808 is more audible. | V — [FUSER Delta example](https://www.masteringthemix.com/blogs/learn/the-fuser-delta-trick-sidechain-ducking-that-doesnt-pump). |
| Trap guidance calls for a few dB of kick-triggered 808 duck and fast-to-medium recovery before the next note; iZotope says use a relatively short release without publishing a universal number. House/EDM sidechain may deliberately pump, so there is no sourced genre-specific duck depth. | V/U — [Louis Romani](https://louisromani.com/blog/how-to-mix-trap-drums), [iZotope 808 example](https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/how-to-mix-808s), [iZotope dance-music example](https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/11-creative-sidechain-compression-techniques). |
| Suggested onset-time starting windows: trap/drill 1–3 dB broadband for 60–150 ms; boom bap/lo-fi 0–2 dB for 60–140 ms (often no duck if onsets separate); house/techno 2–4 dB for 80–180 ms when transparent, up to 6 dB and longer for intentional pumping. | I — composition conventions extrapolated from the 2–3 dB/80–150 ms example and rhythm style, **not measured genre norms**. |
| Keep kick/sub mostly centered below ~100 Hz; 100–200 Hz may retain some width. | V — [Mastering The Mix stereo guidance](https://www.masteringthemix.com/blogs/learn/3-awful-width-mistakes-and-how-to-avoid-them). `pan:0` on a whole bass track is a coarse approximation to frequency-dependent mono. |
| In an EDM kick article, iZotope suggests 100 Hz as a first high-pass point for non-bass hats/vocals/synths. Its pad article has **no universal cutoff** and demonstrates 430 Hz on one drone; its drum article suggests snare 80–200 Hz and hats 300–500 Hz according to source sound. Reverb guidance suggests attenuating below ~200 Hz. | V, all author-specific starting examples — [EDM kick](https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/5-tips-for-balancing-edm-kicks-in-the-mix), [pads](https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/5-tips-for-mixing-pads), [drums](https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/how-to-eq-drums), [reverb](https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/what-is-reverb). |
| Tentative cutoffs, only when a filter exists and the part has no intended low role: pads 150–430 Hz; keys and leads 80–180 Hz (start near 100); hats 250–500 Hz; snare/clap 80–200 Hz; reverb return 150–250 Hz. | I — generalized ranges from the cited examples, **not** a measured instrument table. A snare's body, dark lead, piano left hand, or special effect can warrant lower cutoffs; never hard-fail by instrument name. An HPF can even increase transient peak when near the fundamental ([iZotope mastering example](https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/loud-mix), V). |

## Proposed thresholds for music2

These are **advisory checks**, all I unless identified as an existing measured predicate. Compare only active full-groove/hook or verse windows, not silence, intros, breakdowns, or a single bass note; require a user-selected style or matched reference for genre claims. Static checks inspect expanded events after section overrides, not just pattern strings. Do not hard-fail artistic choices.

| Check and surface | Proposed numeric trigger | Rationale and status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rendered six-band review: `sub+low` | Keep existing warning at **>0.55**, but phrase as “inspect bass/genre reference”; add an optional **>0.70** stronger review for all styles. | 0.55 is existing music2 implementation; 0.70 is I convention. The model alone places bass-heavy cases near or above 0.55, so no generic failure. |
| Rendered sub concentration | If `sub/(sub+low) >0.65` **and** `sub+low >0.50`, ask whether the 808 has enough 60–250 Hz body/harmonics; if `air <0.001`, retain existing empty-air prompt. | I; isolates a sub-heavy imbalance that the combined-low rule misses. Ratios are diagnostic, not published targets. |
| Rendered genre reference | Given ≥3 rights-cleared, level-matched references rendered/analyzed through the **same** music2 pipeline, flag any band >**6 dB** above/below the reference median (`10 log10(share/refShare)`), or >**3 dB** for `sub+low` after listening confirms matching sections. | I tolerance, not measured. The same measurement pipeline is essential; use section metrics and disclose small reference sample. |
| Static kick/bass onsets | If a bass/808 note attacks within **±30 ms** of kick and overlaps the kick for **>80 ms** with `duck.amount <0.11` (~1 dB), prompt a timing/duck/decay review; exclude deliberately unified 808-kick sounds. | I convention from masking advice; event timing alone cannot prove an audible clash. |
| Static kick-triggered duck | For co-occurring kick/bass, suggest **1–3 dB** (`amount=0.11–0.29`) trap/drill, **0–2 dB** (`0–0.21`) boom bap/lo-fi, **2–4 dB** (`0.21–0.37`) transparent house/techno. Flag **>6 dB** (`amount>0.50`) only as possible pumping. | I genre ranges, anchored by the cited FUSER 2–3 dB example. music2 `amount` is fractional amplitude drop: `dB=-20log10(1-amount)` in `src/render/fx.tool.ts`; the FUSER advice is narrow-band, so whole-track duck may need less. |
| Static duck recovery | Suggest music2 `releaseMs` around **35–65 ms** for an 80–150 ms near-recovery goal at 120 BPM; warn if its **~2.3×releaseMs** recovery to <10% deficit exceeds the next kick gap. | I translation of source advice. music2 `releaseMs` is an exponential **time constant**, not time to full return (`src/render/fx.tool.ts`); never copy a compressor's displayed 80–150 ms directly. |
| Static low-end pan and polyphony | Warn when kick/808/sub track has `abs(pan)>0.10`; warn on >**1** simultaneous 808 pitch below C3, excluding designed layers. | I convention; `pan` centers the full track, while `mono` in Song v1 means note-polyphony behavior, not stereo-bass filtering. Rendered mid/side analysis below 100 Hz would be more precise. |
| Static chord voicing | When a bass/808 is active, prompt on same-track chord with **≥2 notes below C3**, or any simultaneous 1–2-semitone pair whose lower note is below E3; prompt on a 3–4-semitone pair below F2, and a 7-semitone pair below C2. | I conservative advisory from the attributed interval list; timbre, inversion, and intentional dissonance override it. Exclude single-note bass tracks. |
| Static relative gain | Only for tracks sharing a known kit/calibrated synth and similar velocity: prompt if bass is **>0 dB** above kick gain, hats **> -5 dB** relative to kick, or pads **> -5 dB** relative to lead. | I coarse arrangement convention. Otherwise JSON `gain` cannot predict stem peak or perceptual level; use rendered per-stem peak/RMS if available. Do not enforce absolute -6 dBFS track peaks from `gain`. |
| Rendered headroom | On an explicitly **unmastered** mix, prompt if sample peak is above **-3 dBFS** or below **-12 dBFS**. Do not apply after music2 ceiling/loudness mastering. | I operating convention from ~6 dB pre-master guidance; the final mastered render can legitimately peak near its ceiling. |
| Instrument low-cut review | Where a future HPF field or stem spectrum is available, suggest the tentative pad/keys/lead/hats/snare/return cutoffs above; flag **>10%** of a non-bass stem's 20–250 Hz power below its suggested cutoff for review. | I experimental. Current Song v1 has no general per-track HPF or isolated reverb-return measurement, so this is **not implementable as reliable JSON lint** now. |

### Limitations to preserve in implementation

`LOW_END_DOMINANCE` and `EMPTY_HIGH_BAND` are analysis prompts, not genre certificates. A 20–60 Hz share depends strongly on song key, kick fundamental, FFT/binning, section occupancy, and mastered processing. Some primary sources show qualitative differences but no public band coordinates for the named subgenres; a measured genre table would require a documented, licensed corpus analyzed with music2's own six-band method. No source supports making the proposed intervals, high-pass frequencies, relative fader values, or sidechain settings hard validation errors.
