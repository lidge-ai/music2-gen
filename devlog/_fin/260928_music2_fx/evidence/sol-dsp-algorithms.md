# Offline DSP algorithms: clean-room implementation notes

Scope: `Float32Array` planar stereo at `Fs = 44_100 | 48_000`, single-threaded offline rendering. All equations below are descriptions of published methods plus proposed music2 parameter conventions, not copied program code. A 3-minute song has 15.876 M or 17.280 M stereo frames respectively; CPU estimates below are **48 kHz arithmetic-operation budgets**, not wall-clock promises (44.1 kHz is 0.919×). Count one multiply/add/compare as roughly one operation, exclude allocation and transcendental setup unless noted. Runtime dependencies: zero. Compute coefficients and state in JS double precision, write audio to `Float32Array`; keep separate state per channel, process frames in fixed order, preallocate rings, and keep the fixed song seed/`src/shared/prng.tool.ts` for any initial phase or randomized design choice. No `Math.random`/clock in DSP. Clamp or reject NaN/infinite inputs and parameter values before processing; reset a nonfinite state and report an error rather than silently propagating it. Tests should run at both rates with finite output, zero-input/zero-state silence, chunk-size equivalence and same-seed bitwise replay on the same Node major/platform.

**Source rights:** W3C Audio EQ Cookbook is a W3C Working Group Note under its permissive document license. Other linked articles/books/theses are *published, copyrighted papers or instructional material*, not public-domain software licenses; equations and independently derived implementations are the research basis, not permission to copy prose, code, figures, or patented implementation. Smith's online book is likewise copyrighted. No AGPL/GPL implementation is a source here. Suggested numeric defaults and tolerances are music2 engineering choices unless explicitly labeled as a paper default.

## 1. RBJ Audio EQ Cookbook biquads, transposed direct form II

Source/status: [W3C Audio EQ Cookbook](https://www.w3.org/TR/audio-eq-cookbook/) (permissive W3C document; adapted from RBJ with permission). Design in double: `w=2πf/Fs`, `c=cos(w)`, `s=sin(w)`, `A=10^(gainDb/40)`, `a=s/(2Q)` for LP/HP/BP/peak; shelves use `a=s/2 * sqrt((A+1/A)(1/S-1)+2)`, `B=2sqrt(A)a`. Coefficient tuples below are unnormalized `(b0,b1,b2;a0,a1,a2)`; divide every coefficient by `a0` once, then `y=b0*x+z1; z1=b1*x-a1*y+z2; z2=b2*x-a2*y` (two states/channel). Source BP variant chosen: **constant 0 dB peak**, not constant skirt gain.

| Kind | `b0,b1,b2` | `a0,a1,a2` |
|---|---|---|
| LP | `(1-c)/2, 1-c, (1-c)/2` | `1+a, -2c, 1-a` |
| HP | `(1+c)/2, -(1+c), (1+c)/2` | same |
| BP | `a, 0, -a` | same |
| Peak | `1+aA, -2c, 1-aA` | `1+a/A, -2c, 1-a/A` |
| Low shelf | `A[(A+1)-(A-1)c+B], 2A[(A-1)-(A+1)c], A[(A+1)-(A-1)c-B]` | `(A+1)+(A-1)c+B, -2[(A-1)+(A+1)c], (A+1)+(A-1)c-B` |
| High shelf | `A[(A+1)+(A-1)c+B], -2A[(A-1)+(A+1)c], A[(A+1)+(A-1)c-B]` | `(A+1)-(A-1)c+B, 2[(A-1)-(A+1)c], (A+1)-(A-1)c-B` |

Proposed range/default: `f=20..min(20_000,0.45Fs)`/1 kHz, `Q=0.25..20`/`1/√2`, peak/shelf gain `-24..24`/0 dB, shelf `S=0.1..1`/1. Do not switch coefficients abruptly: interpolate parameters at control rate and recalculate, or use §2 for audio-rate sweeps. Cost ≈20 ops/stereo frame per section, ≈346 M ops/3 min. Oracle: 1 kHz LP/HP at `Q=1/√2` read `-3.010±0.05 dB` at 1 kHz; BP 0±0.05 dB at center; +6 dB peak center and +6 dB shelf plateau within 0.1 dB; +6 then -6 dB matched peak cascade returns impulse within `2e-4` sample amplitude.

## 2. Zavalishin TPT state-variable filter (SVF)

Source/status: [Zavalishin, *The Art of VA Filter Design*, ch. 5](https://www.discodsp.net/VAFilterDesign.pdf) (publicly readable copyrighted book, not public domain). Trapezoidal integrators retain topology while `f` changes. Per channel, `g=tan(πf/Fs)`, `k=1/Q`, `h=(x-(k+g)s1-s2)/(1+kg+g²)`, `bp=g*h+s1`, `lp=g*bp+s2`, `s1=2bp-s1`, `s2=2lp-s2`; `hp=h`, optional unity-peak BP=`k*bp`. Recompute `g` per sample if modulated, ideally via control-rate linear interpolation of **log cutoff** and per-sample `tan`; do not interpolate internal states or let `f` hit Nyquist. Range/default `f=20..0.45Fs`/1 kHz; `Q=0.5..12`/`1/√2`, with `k≥1/12`; LFO 0.01–20 Hz, depth 0–4 octaves. Positive damping keeps stable poles even for high Q, but high-Q step transients can be loud: guard headroom and input, and smooth discontinuous controls. Cost ≈25 ops + one `tan`/stereo frame, ≈432 M ops + 17.28 M `tan` calls for shared cutoff. Oracle: impulse response at 1 kHz, `Q=1/√2` gives LP `-3.01±0.1 dB` at 1 kHz; sweep `f=50→18 kHz→50` over 1 s with `Q=12`, bounded 0.1 input yields finite output and no exponentially growing post-sweep tail; same sweep split into blocks is sample-identical.

## 3. Reverb: Dattorro plate primary; Jot-style FDN room alternative

Sources/status: [Dattorro, *Effect Design Part 1*, JAES 1997, Fig. 1 and Tables 1–2](https://www.convexoptimization.com/TOOLS/EffectDesignPart1.pdf) (published copyrighted paper); [Jot/Chaigne 1991 AES bibliographic page](https://aes.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=5663) (published copyrighted paper, access may require subscription); [public tutorial deriving FDN absorption law](https://www-sop.inria.fr/reves/personnel/Nicolas.Tsingos/publis/sig02_tutorial.pdf) (published copyrighted tutorial). Use Dattorro as the primary `plate`/lush `hall` preset; 8×8 FDN has fewer diffusion stages and is the cheaper `room` preset. These are design recommendations, not claims that the topologies have identical sound.

**Plate structure.** Mono sum `u=(L+R)/2`, optional circular pre-delay, input one-pole bandwidth LP, four serial Schroeder allpasses, then inject into two crossed figure-eight tank halves; each half has a modulated early allpass, long delay, damping LP, late allpass, and delay. An allpass with delay `D` and coefficient `a` is `v=x-a*ring[D]`, `y=ring[D]+a*v`, `ringWrite=v`; `|a|<1`. Published reference is **Fs=29,761 Hz**; use input delays `[142,107,379,277]`, early tank APs `[672,908]`, long delays `[4453,4217]`, late APs `[1800,2656]`, final delays `[3720,3163]` samples as the Fig. 1 layout. At 44.1/48 kHz scale every fixed delay and Table 2 output tap index by `round(N*Fs/29761)`, retaining relative topology; floor at one sample and allocate maximum LFO excursion. The first tank AP pair modulates around scaled `672,908` with independent quadrature sine phases; reference excursion 16 samples at 29,761 Hz and modulation about 1 Hz. Read fractional positions with §5's interpolation. Table 2's seven signed taps per side are mixed with factor 0.6 (apply headroom/normalization before external wet mix); its indices and signs should be transcribed from the paper table, scaled by the same ratio, and kept as a golden test fixture. Do **not** substitute one tank terminal sample for the published multitap output.

Table 2 tap fixture, in its 29,761-Hz sample coordinates (node names denote figure segments; scale indices with the delays): `YL=.6*(+node48_54[266]+node48_54[2974]-node55_59[1913]+node59_63[1996]-node24_30[1990]-node31_33[187]-node33_39[1066])`; `YR=.6*(+node24_30[353]+node24_30[3627]-node31_33[1228]+node33_39[2673]-node48_54[2111]-node55_59[335]-node59_63[121])`. The first left tap is printed as `266` in the source scan; verify this one visually against the PDF figure before coding because text extraction is ambiguous. Do **not** substitute one tank terminal sample for the published multitap output.

Proposed controls: predelay 0–200/20 ms, input bandwidth LP cutoff 2–20/16 kHz, input diffusion coefficients 0–0.8/`[.75,.75,.625,.625]`, tank early/late diffusion 0–0.8/`.7,.5`, decay knob 0.1–0.95/0.5, damping LP 2–20/8 kHz, wet 0–1/0.2. Dattorro's actual Table 1 defaults are decay `.50`, early/late diffusion `.70/.50`, input diffusion `.750/.625`, bandwidth `.9995`, damping `.0005`; its latter two are paper coefficients, **not Hz controls**. For an `RT60` UI (0.2–10/2 s), calibrate an impulse at each rate and solve the global decay scalar by bisection; an initial estimate is `g≈10^(-3*Tloop/RT60)` using the mean relevant loop traversal time. Because AP/damping/taps affect measured decay, never claim this estimate exact. Pre-delay does not belong in the feedback loop. Cost roughly 100–160 arithmetic ops/stereo frame plus 2 interpolated reads, ≈1.7–2.8 G ops/3 min; ring storage ≈100 KiB per 29,761-Hz topology scaled to Fs, plus pre-delay. Oracle: zero-modulation impulse first wet sample must occur after pre-delay and diffusion latency; measure EDC `E[n]=Σ_{k≥n}(L[k]²+R[k]²)` and fit `10log10(E)` between -5 and -35 dB, inferred RT60 within ±20% of calibrated request at 44.1 and 48 kHz; silent tail decays and never diverges for max decay/damping.

**FDN room.** Eight incommensurate delays, e.g. `[1013,1201,1429,1699,1999,2371,2801,3313]` samples at 48 kHz (music2 design values, scaled in seconds at 44.1 kHz, adjust collisions), two decorrelated input/output sign vectors, and normalized 8×8 Hadamard feedback `H/√8`. Use three butterfly passes per sample, not 64 scalar products. If `d_i` are line outputs, `v=H d/√8`, `write_i=b_i*u+absorb_i(v_i)`, `wetL/R=Σ c_{L/R,i}d_i/√8`; choose vectors so left/right differ and normalized magnitude remains controlled. For desired `T60(f)`, line gain `G_i(f)=10^(-3 D_i/(Fs*T60(f)))` (Jot law). A one-pole absorption `H_i(z)=b_i/(1-p_i z^-1)` matches target DC and Nyquist gains `G0,Gπ`: `p=(G0-Gπ)/(G0+Gπ)`, `b=2G0Gπ/(G0+Gπ)`, with `Gπ≤G0≤1`; this approximates a smooth two-band decay, not arbitrary RT60 curves. Propose `T60_low=.2..4/0.7 s`, `T60_high=.1..2/0.35 s`, predelay 0–100/10 ms; optional two short input allpasses for denser early echoes. Ensure every filter magnitude <1 and butterfly normalization exact; do not seed random lengths at render time. Cost ≈80–110 ops/stereo frame, ≈1.4–1.9 G ops/3 min; 8 rings ≈116 KiB at 48 kHz. Oracle: with damping off and target 1 s, impulse EDC fit at low/mid frequencies 1.0±0.15 s; with `T60_high=.5 s`, high-band EDC decays faster than low-band; no line rings forever.

## 4. Tempo-synced stereo feedback delay

Source/status: [Smith, feedback/filtered comb description](https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/pasp/Comb_Filters.html) (copyrighted online textbook). At BPM `B`, note fraction `p/q` of a whole note and optional dot/triplet multiplier `m`, `D=round(Fs*240*p*m/(B*q))`. Ring read before write: `eL=bufL[n-D]`, `eR=bufR[n-D]`; loop HP then LP on each echo (`hp=e-lpHP(e)`, `lp=a*prev+(1-a)*hp`, `a=exp(-2πfc/Fs)`), `writeL=xL+g*filter(eR)` and `writeR=xR+g*filter(eL)` for ping-pong; straight stereo uses same-side echoes. Output `dry*x+wet*e`; use equal-power wet/dry only as an optional mix policy. Range/default BPM 40–240/120, division 1/1–1/64/1/8 dotted, feedback `0..0.95/.35`, HP `20..2000/150 Hz`, LP `1000..20000/8000 Hz`, wet `0..1/.25`; 10-s max ring guard. Smooth time changes with dual read heads/crossfade or reject tempo automation at audio rate. Feedback stability requires `|g|<1` and loop filter gain ≤1. Cost ≈25–35 ops/stereo frame, ≈430–605 M ops/3 min. Oracle: at 120 BPM, eighth note has `D=0.25Fs`, first impulse echo at D±0 samples, ping-pong alternates channels, echo amplitude `g^k` with filters bypassed ±1e-5; zero input with existing state decays.

## 5. Chorus/flanger, fractional delay

Sources/status: [Smith, time-varying delay effects](https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/pasp/Time_Varying_Delay_Effects.html), [Laakso et al., fractional delay survey](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/splitting-the-unit-delay-tools-for-fractional-delay-filter-design/) (copyrighted textbook/paper). `D_c[n]=Fs*(baseMs+depthMs*sin(2πrate*n/Fs+φ_c))/1000`, wet delayed sample from one ring/channel, output `dry*x+wet*y`; `φR=φL+π/2` by default. Chorus base 10–35/20 ms, depth 1–10/5 ms, rate .05–5/.35 Hz, wet .1–.8/.35, feedback 0; flanger base .5–5/2 ms, depth .1–2/1 ms, rate .02–5/.25 Hz, feedback -.9..+.9/.4. Constrain `base>depth+4/Fs` for interpolator guard. Linear read at `D=m+μ`: `(1-μ)x[n-m]+μx[n-m-1]`; 3rd-order Lagrange weights on neighbors `j=-1..2`: `w_j=Π_{k≠j}(μ-k)/(j-k)` with indexing chosen consistently (4 reads; flatter magnitude but more CPU). First-order allpass fractional section may conserve magnitude but its time-varying state changes phase; prefer Lagrange for chorus, linear for cheap flange. Smooth parameter changes; `|feedback|<1`, no buffer allocation/sample. Cost ≈20–35 ops/stereo frame linear or ≈45–65 Lagrange, ≈345–605 M / 778 M–1.12 G ops/3 min, plus sine (use shared phase accumulator/control-rate table if measured cheaper). Oracle: frozen `D=100.5`, impulse has exactly two `.5` linear tap samples; fixed flanger `D=2 ms`, equal dry/wet yields comb maxima at integer `1/D` Hz and nulls `(k+.5)/D` ±1 FFT bin; stereo phase offset reproduces fixed snapshot.

## 6. Phaser

Source/status: [Smith, virtual-analog phasing](https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/pasp/Virtual_Analog_Example_Phasing.html) (copyrighted textbook). Cascade 4 or 6 first-order allpasses per channel: `a=(tan(πf/Fs)-1)/(tan(πf/Fs)+1)`, `y=a*x+z`, `z=x-a*y` (equivalent `H=(a+z^-1)/(1+a z^-1)`); each stage gets a geometrically spaced cutoff around LFO-controlled center, e.g. `f_i=clamp(fcenter*2^((i-(N-1)/2)*0.6),50,8000)`. Mix `out=.5*(dry+processed)`; feedback `xStage=x+g*lastProcessed`, `|g|<.9`. LFO .05–5/.4 Hz, sweep center 150–3000/500 Hz, depth 0–3/1.5 octaves, stages 4/4, feedback -.8..+.8/0, wet 0..1/.5. Coefficient updates at 64-sample control intervals with interpolation; clamp `|a|<.9999`; allpass alone preserves magnitude, mixing creates moving notches. Cost ≈45–70 ops/stereo frame for four stages, ≈778 M–1.21 G ops/3 min plus coefficient trig. Oracle: any fixed cutoff, allpass-only impulse transfer magnitude 0±0.05 dB at 100, 1k, 10k Hz; wet/dry mix has at least one notch ≥10 dB below adjacent passbands and remains bounded under max LFO/feedback.

## 7. Feed-forward compressor with sidechain

Source/status: [Giannoulis, Massberg & Reiss 2012, AES](https://aes.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=16354) (copyrighted JAES paper, not a code license). Detector on optional aligned sidechain stereo pair (else input): `u=max(|scL|,|scR|)` for linked peak, `xDb=20log10(max(u,1e-12))`; stereo audio gain remains linked. Let `d=xDb-threshold`, ratio `R≥1`, knee width `W≥0`, slope `s=1-1/R`. Desired positive reduction `r=0` if `d≤-W/2`, `r=s*d` if `d≥W/2`, else `r=s*(d+W/2)^2/(2W)`; for W=0 use hard branch. Smooth **positive reduction in dB after gain computer**: `α=exp(-1/(τFs))`, choose attack if `r>state`, release otherwise, `state=α*state+(1-α)*r`; gain `10^((makeupDb-state)/20)`, apply to both audio channels. This is a branch-smooth peak detector/gain envelope; no lookahead, so fast transients may pass. Range/default threshold -60..0/-18 dBFS, ratio 1..20/4, knee 0..24/6 dB, attack .1..100/10 ms, release 10..2000/150 ms, makeup -12..24/0 dB. If sidechain length differs, reject or pad deterministically by explicit policy. Bound dB floor, no `log10(0)`; optional gain ceiling. Cost ≈30 ops plus one log and one exp/stereo frame, ≈518 M ops + 34.56 M transcendentals/3 min. Oracle: sustained -12 dBFS sidechain, T=-18, R=4, W=0 leads to -4.5±0.05 dB reduction after 10 attacks; step onset reaches 63.2±1% of target reduction at one attack τ, release falls to 36.8±1% after one release τ; identical sidechain drives identical gain L/R.

## 8. Saturation and deterministic oversampling

Sources/status: [Smith, soft clipping and even harmonics](https://dsprelated.com/freebooks/pasp/Nonlinear_Distortion.html), [Smith, window-method FIR](https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/sasp/Window_Method_FIR_Filter.html) (copyrighted online textbooks). Shapers with drive `d=10^(driveDb/20)`: tanh `f(x)=tanh(dx)/tanh(d)`; cubic `v=clamp(dx,-1,1)`, `f(v)=1.5v-.5v³` (unit output); asymmetric tube-*style* `f(x)=tanh(d(x+b))-tanh(db)` then divide by a chosen peak/positive-negative normalization, plus DC blocker `y=x-prevX+r*prevY`, `r≈.995` (no physical tube-model claim). Drive 0–24/6 dB, bias -0.3..0.3/0.1, mix 0..1/.5, oversample 1/2/4, default 2× for tanh/asymmetry; 1× is acceptable for mild cubic. For 2× or 4×, cascade 2× stages: design odd-length symmetric half-band low-pass once at init by `h[k]=2fc*sinc(2fc(k-M/2))*w[k]`, `fc=.25` cycles per **high-rate** sample, Hamming `w=.54-.46cos(2πk/M)`, normalize `Σh=1`; every non-center even offset is zero before windowing. Use 31–63 taps/2× stage, polyphase zero-stuff interpolation with gain 2, then filter/decimate after nonlinear stage. Stage count and normalization must be tested; up/down FIR group delays must be compensated in dry mix or delay dry. Cache taps per Fs/quality; exploit zero taps/polyphase and oversample **only nonlinear insert/bus**, not full arrangement. Poly `x³` introduces harmonics to 3× frequency, tanh more; 4× helps but does not make arbitrary drive alias-free. Cost per 3-minute stereo insert: 1× cubic ≈200 M arithmetic ops; 2× 31-tap polyphase pair roughly 2–4 G ops, 4× roughly 5–10 G (implementation dependent), plus 2×/4× tanh calls; benchmark before enabling 4× by default. Oracle: f(x) odd for tanh/cubic, asymmetric bias creates measurable second harmonic but zero-input DC settles below -80 dBFS; 1 kHz cubic x=.5 matches equation ±1e-6; oversampled 10 kHz high-drive sine has >10 dB less strongest in-band alias than 1×, level-corrected passband 1 kHz within .2 dB, and reported latency equals FIR path delay.

## 9. Bitcrusher/sample-rate reducer

Source/status: [Smith, quantization step and round-off](https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/mdft/Round_Off_Error_Variance.html) (copyrighted textbook; basic arithmetic derivation); [NIST PCM glossary](https://www.nist.gov/glossary-term/28881) (public government definition, page reuse terms separate from public-domain algorithm). Signed uniform midtread quantizer on clamped `v∈[-1,1]`: `Q_b(v)=round(v*(2^(b-1)-1))/(2^(b-1)-1)` for `b≥2`. Integer hold length `H=max(1,round(Fs/targetHz))`; every H frames, capture/quantize current input to held L/R, repeat between updates (explicitly aliasing by design). Bits 2–16/8, targetHz 1–Fs/11,025, wet 0..1/1; shared frame counter but separate held channels, optionally reset hold at note/clip boundaries by documented choice. Optional TPDF dither uses two draws from seeded PRNG and amplitude 1 LSB, off by default. Keep exact silence 0, prevent 1-bit denominator, update H only at capture boundaries. Cost ≈8 ops/stereo frame, ≈138 M ops/3 min. Oracle: at b=4, input 0.5 maps to `4/7`; H=4, impulse at frame 0 is held for exactly frames 0..3 then zero; same seed yields identical dither sequence.

## 10. Mid/side width, mono below cutoff, Haas

Source/status: [Linkwitz's filter notes](https://www.linkwitzlab.com/filters.htm) (copyrighted public technical page), [RBJ/W3C coefficients](https://www.w3.org/TR/audio-eq-cookbook/) (permissive spec). `M=(L+R)/2`, `S=(L-R)/2`; split S with LR4 at cutoff via two cascaded Butterworth (`Q=1/√2`) LPs and two HPs. `S'=width*HP4(S)+lowWidth*LP4(S)`; mono below cutoff sets `lowWidth=0`; reconstruct `L'=M+S'`, `R'=M-S'`. Range/default width 0..2/1, cutoff 50..500/120 Hz, lowWidth 0..1/0. For strict full-band reconstruction with width=1 and lowWidth=1, use `S'=S` bypass rather than adding LR branches to dry M, because LR4 summation has crossover phase; provide a phase-matched M crossover if constant amplitude/phase reconstruction is required. Haas: optional delay **one side's highpassed side content** 0–25/0 ms, never low-frequency M; keep delayed branch level low, e.g. -9 dB, and compensate wet latency as needed. Haas can cancel in mono: test and offer mono-safe default 0. Cost width-only ≈8 ops/stereo frame (138 M/3 min), LR4 ≈90 ops/frame (1.56 G), Haas ring adds ≈5 ops/frame. Oracle: width 0 yields `L=R=M` exactly; mono-below 120 Hz attenuates 50 Hz side by ≥25 dB while 1 kHz side stays within 0.2 dB of requested gain; LR4 individual LP/HP outputs -6.02±0.1 dB at 120 Hz; Haas 10 ms impulse offset exactly `round(.01Fs)` samples.

## 11. PolyBLEP saw/square and seven-voice supersaw

Sources/status: [Välimäki & Huovilainen 2007 research record](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/antialiasing-oscillators-in-subtractive-synthesis/) (copyrighted IEEE paper); [Szabo, *How to Emulate the Super Saw*, 2010](https://www.adamszabo.com/internet/adam_szabo_how_to_emulate_the_super_saw.pdf) (publicly readable copyrighted thesis). With phase `t∈[0,1)` and increment `dt=f/Fs`, `blep(t,dt)=2u-u²-1` for `t<dt`, `u=t/dt`; `=u²+2u+1` for `t>1-dt`, `u=(t-1)/dt`; else 0. Saw `2t-1-blep(t,dt)`; pulse `1` if `t<pw` else `-1`, then `+blep(t,dt)-blep((t-pw+1)%1,dt)` (check edge sign against chosen saw polarity). Advance/wrap phase **after** evaluating sample, `t=(t+dt)%1`; require `f≤0.45Fs` and `pw∈[2dt,1-2dt]` so edges do not overlap. Duty .1–.9/.5, saw/square amplitude 0..1/.5. PolyBLEP reduces aliasing but is not perfectly bandlimited near Nyquist; optionally bandlimit with mipmapped wavetables for high notes.

Supersaw: seven independent PolyBLEP saws, center `f`, six `f_i=f*(1+o_i*D(x))` with Szabo's measured full-scale offsets `[-.11002313,-.06288439,-.01952356,+.01991221,+.06216538,+.10745242]`. For normalized detune `x∈[0,1]`, published 11th-order fit `D(x)=10028.7312891634x^11-50818.8652045924x^10+111363.4808729368x^9-138150.6761080548x^8+106649.6679158292x^7-53046.9642751875x^6+17019.9518580080x^5-3425.0836591318x^4+404.2703938388x^3-24.1878824391x²+.6717417634x+.0030115596`; clamp D to 0..1 (the fit's small x=0 offset is an approximation artifact). Published mix fits at `m∈[0,1]`: center `C=.99785-.55366m`, each side `V=-.73764m²+1.2841m+.044372`; normalize by `C+6V` for peak-conservative level or sqrt(`C²+6V²`) for energy, document chosen policy. Defaults detune .5, mix .5; initialize seven phases from the song's seeded PRNG **once per note/voice**, not each sample; offset L/R voice placement in balanced pairs if stereo desired. Evaluate the high-order fit in double/Horner at note/control changes, never per sample; clamp frequency below Nyquist. Cost one PolyBLEP saw ≈20–30 ops/sample (≈345–518 M ops/3-min mono voice); seven ≈140–210 ops/sample (≈2.4–3.6 G ops/voice/3 min), excluding downstream stereo panning. Oracle: dt=.01 saw never creates an uncompensated 2-unit edge jump; naive saw vs PolyBLEP 8 kHz at Fs=44.1 kHz shows ≥10 dB lower strongest aliased FFT line; Szabo offsets at detune=1 and center/side gains at mix=.5 match the thesis within 1e-4 before normalization; fixed seed gives bit-identical phase starts.

## 12. Tremolo/autopan

Source/status: [Smith, time-varying delay effects/amplitude modulation](https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/pasp/Time_Varying_Delay_Effects.html) (copyrighted textbook; elementary sinusoidal modulation). Shared phase `θ[n+1]=(θ[n]+2πrate/Fs) mod 2π`; tremolo `g[n]=(1-depth)+depth*(1+sin θ)/2`, `outL/R=g*inL/R`. Autopan for mono source `p=sin θ`, equal-power `gL=cos(π(p+1)/4)`, `gR=sin(π(p+1)/4)`; stereo mode cross-pan or apply inverse gains while preserving sum at center, with explicit policy. Rate .01–20/4 Hz or tempo division 1/1–1/32/1/4, depth 0..1/.5, phase 0..2π/0, mix 0..1/1. Sin/cos may be computed per sample or through a deterministic phase table; no PRNG needed. Cost tremolo ≈8 ops + sine/stereo frame (≈138 M ops + 17.28 M sines), pan ≈10 ops + trig (≈173 M ops + trigs). Oracle: depth 0 is bitwise bypass; depth 1 tremolo of DC 1 has min/max 0/1 ±1e-5 and mean .5±.001 over whole cycles; equal-power pan has `gL²+gR²=1±1e-6` at all phases.

## 13. ADSR envelope filter on TPT SVF cutoff

Source/status: [Zavalishin TPT SVF](https://www.discodsp.net/VAFilterDesign.pdf) (copyrighted book); ADSR segmentation is a music2 design convention. Per voice/note, envelope `e∈[0,1]` has attack `0→1` in A seconds, decay `1→sustain` in D, hold while gate on, and release from **current** `e` to 0 in R (capture `eRelease` at note-off). For linear segments, `e[n]=eStart+(eEnd-eStart)*min(1,(n-nStart)/N)` where `N=max(1,round(seconds*Fs))`; no block-dependent counters. `f[n]=clamp(baseHz*2^(depthOct*e[n]+lfoOct[n]),20,0.45Fs)`, feed §2's per-sample `g=tan(πf/Fs)`; optional negative depth closes rather than opens. Range/default A .001–5/.01 s, D .001–5/.2 s, sustain 0..1/.5, R .001–10/.3 s, base 20–10000/300 Hz, depth -6..+6/+3 octaves, Q .5..12/1; retrigger policy resets attack from current e to avoid discontinuity. Cost ≈10 ops + pow/tan per active voice sample in addition to §2, ≈173 M ops + 17.28 M each transcendental for one 3-min continuously active voice (typically far less due to note durations). Oracle: at Fs=48 kHz, A=.01 s, e reaches 1 on exactly frame 480 under the chosen endpoint convention; note-off during decay releases continuously; base 300/depth +3 opens to 2400 Hz at e=1; two render blockings produce identical buffer.
