# Reading `overview.png`

Open the image after `music2 analyze`. Read the time panels from left to right; the right rail lists the section occurrences in the same order. Bar labels are 1-based. The last printed boundary is the bar after the final song bar, and a render tail lies outside this song axis.

1. **Header (top):** title, duration, meter or axis, BPM, key, whole-file integrated LUFS, and true peak. `N/A` means the value was unavailable. The whole-file LUFS is not a section score.
2. **Three verdict slots:** the first selects a warning such as clipping or off-target loudness, or `NO FLAGS`. The second reports the largest adjacent section loudness gap, or `SECTION GAP N/A`. The third names the first declared hook, or `NO HOOK DECLARED`. Check the detailed warning and flow records in `analysis.json` or `analysis.md` before changing the song.
3. **Section flow and rail:** proportional blocks show occurrence number, role, and printed `id#occurrence`. The rail repeats each occurrence on two lines, for example `02 HOOK#0 B05 0:07` and `SECTION LUFS -12.4`. The first occurrence suffix is `#0`; the `B05` start is bar 5. Read the final `B(N+1)` tick as an end boundary. An orange outline marks a hook. `LOUDEST` marks the greatest finite, gated **section integrated LUFS** value in the rail; it does not mean the highest point on the curve. With more than 20 occurrences, 50 px rail rows extend the image height.
4. **Loudness:** the sky-blue per-bar/interval curve uses ungated LUFS and a short-term trace. Its shape shows progression. Compare adjacent sections by their signed gap in LU; do not substitute a curve point for the gated `SECTION LUFS` table value.
5. **Arrangement density:** for song-backed analysis, DRUMS, BASS/808, and OTHER NOTES lanes show event counts as bar heights, labeled `EVENTS/BAR`. More events do not necessarily sound louder. WAV-only analysis shows onset density per second without track claims.
6. **Music2 RGB waveform:** red is approximately below 200 Hz, green about 200–2000 Hz, and blue above 2000 Hz. The filters overlap at both crossovers, so the colors do not sum to an exact full-band energy partition. The silhouette shows peaks and the darker inset shows RMS.
7. **Novelty:** marked peaks indicate measured changes. `Bxx HIT` means a detected peak fell within one bar of a declared section boundary; `MISS` means it did not. The plot scales to its own maximum, printed as `MAX x.xx`. A missing peak does not prove that a declared change is inaudible: short repeating patterns can hide a boundary from this detector.
8. **Brightness:** the line is spectral centroid in Hz. A higher centroid suggests more high-frequency weight; it is not a loudness reading. Gaps mean no centroid was measured.
9. **Global band share and similarity inset:** the six `GLOBAL BAND SHARE — NOT TIME` bars are whole-file percentages, not a timeline. The adjacent self-similarity matrix uses mean-centered interval features; pale off-diagonal blocks or stripes can indicate repeated passages. It is drawn at up to 256 px, while repeat detection uses the full matrix. Display contrast stretches from the printed `DARK = x.xx` floor to `PALE = 1.00`; that stretch does not change novelty or repeat thresholds. Repeat labels give the two matching bar ranges, or seconds for WAV-only input.
10. **Legend:** number refers to the section table, orange outline to hook, sky blue to LUFS, and bar height to events per bar. Read labels and axes even if colors are unavailable.

For WAV-only input, the time axis says `BEATS` or `0.5 S` and the rail says `NO DECLARED SECTIONS`. Beat ticks are not song bars; no section order, declared boundary, or hook is claimed. For actionable level and band adjustments, see [mixing.md](mixing.md). Images and metrics cannot establish that an agent heard the audio.
